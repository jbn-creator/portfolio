  • Predicting Power-Grid Outages with Deep LearningOngoing Forecasting grid failure from long, noisy temporal signals. Supervised by Dr Shirin Dora.

    Reliable early forecasting matters because it lets operators take preemptive action — switching to backup systems, redirecting load, or simply preparing. The work draws on LSTMs and RNNs, which are well-suited to the long, noisy temporal patterns that grid signals exhibit, and explores how the framing of the prediction task itself shapes how well a model can learn it.

  • BattlefieldOngoing A multi-agent RL environment where countries compete over capital, land, people and oil.

    Battlefield is a multi-agent reinforcement learning environment where countries act as agents competing over resources under constraints that loosely model international competition. The project explores what strategies emerge when agents with different priors and reward weightings interact in a shared zero-sum economy.

    The motivating question came from listening to Jiang Xuequin's game-theoretic analyses of geopolitics: if states really were independent agents maximising under a zero-sum game, what would optimal play look like, and would it resemble anything we recognise? The honest answer is probably no — the environment is far too simple to model real strategic behaviour. But the simpler question is interesting in itself: what does multi-agent RL converge to under these constraints, and how do those equilibria shift as you change the resource structure or the reward shape, if they do at all?
